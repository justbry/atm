## Review

All eight slices were reviewed by sub-agents and the findings fixed slice by slice (see the PRs for each fix). Decisions that came out of it:

- **Handler `schoolId` binding.** The webhook handler's deps carry a required `schoolId`, the school an endpoint and its secret belong to. An event naming another school is refused (422 `school_mismatch`); an event with no `b4u_school` at all is answered 200 `ignored`; half-filled metadata stays 422 `missing_metadata`; an unmapped event type answers 200 `ignored`; a payment linked to more than one invoice answers 422 `ambiguous_payment`.
- **Alerts only for `track` and `finalize` draws.** `credit.low` and `credit.depleted` follow customer activity (holds and spends), not refund or dispute clawbacks or admin edits. They are judged on availability including holds. atm does not remember it already alerted, so the family service dedupes per holder, feature and grant or cycle.
- **`FolderStore` reserved names are case-insensitive**, and both object stores validate keys and ranges more strictly.
- **Equal-second membership ordering.** Membership events that carry the same second now have a defined order (see the PR for the rule).
- **`deterministicUlid` is exported** so a caller can derive `eventId` as the handler does.
- **Replay of the log works** (#29, merged into `dev`). It had failed even on a plain create, renew, cancel of a one-feature tier. Three causes, three fixes: event ids are time-based, so a `reset` at the period start sorted before the `applyBillingPlan` at payment time (replay now runs each subject's commands in revision order, `replayOrder`); commands that moved no credits were not logged (each is now a `plan.changed` with its command and mutation, and every handler event carries `payload.complete`); and each per-feature event recorded only its own slice of the mutation (every event now records the whole mutation, with `payload.slice` naming the changes it is made from). The replay golden gained the `plan.changed` events; the envelope is unchanged; the `credit.*` payload schemas gain optional `slice` and `complete`. Owner decisions: `payload.mutation.changes` holds all changes and a reader uses `payload.slice`; `plan.changed` for every command that moves no credits (no `plan.attached`); replay tolerates a revision gap in a log whose writer marked `complete` (logging grants-only writes would close it). **Logs written by 0.3.0** are read, not rewritten: mutations are reassembled by `mutation.id` and checked as a subset of what the engine decides; a log with a revision gap stops with `ReplayGapError` (the lost cycle-end update cannot be told from a grants-only write); a log without one replays and reports `lossySubjects`. What remains: a 0.3.0 log cannot be rebuilt past its first gap. Closing that needs the stored rows at that revision (the handler's `atm_rows`) or a one-off migration that appends the missing commands, neither built.

## Possible directions, not planned

### Usage invoicing
- Metered usage pricing is out of scope; atm is a credits and flat-subscription engine. Listed so the decision stays visible.
- What it would be: events become a quantity per metric, the quantity is priced (unit, tiered, volume, package), the result is an invoice. Late events need a grace window; the vendors differ (Orb 12 hours, Stripe 1 hour up to 72, Metronome a 34-day backdating window).
- Open decision: pass usage to Stripe meters, or rate it in atm. The first keeps Stripe the system of record for money; the second makes atm compete with Stripe Billing.

Agentlake sums numbers in labels as `::DOUBLE`, which drifts. Do not put usage decimals in postings: postings are 2-place USD and `format: decimal` maps to `DECIMAL(18,2)`.

Grok's reasoning-in-output relationship and its 10^10 ticks to the dollar are unconfirmed, and a mapping cannot fix a provider whose docs are silent.

- **Spike 2, R2 pipeline: yes for the raw store, aggregation, rating and re-rating; partly for the existing outbox and shipper, which do not scale to this volume.** Branch `claude/atm-usage-pipeline-spike` (head `e292cbe2b`, not merged, not a PR): a generator, SQL and manifest builder as test helpers under `tests/spikes/` plus 11 tests; the full suite, `tsc`, Biome and `knip` passed on it. Measured on an Apple M5 Pro (15 cores, 48 GB), DuckDB 1.5.6, 20,000 Zipf-skewed holders (top 10 carry 39%), with 1% duplicates and about 1% late events (1 to 40 days old) on top; DuckDB read from a warm page cache (a cold disk was not measured), and about two thirds of the numbers are a single run with roughly +-40% variance.
  - **Bytes per stored event:** raw JSONL (the outbox body) 456; gzip or zstd JSONL 63 to 70; Parquet with the full envelope and the payload as JSON 45; Parquet with promoted typed columns 47; flat Parquet 28.7. `idem` (14.5 B) and `id` (13 B) are random and dominate; `id` is derivable from school, `idem` and event time. Sorting by holder made the file larger (48.5 against 45); time order is better. R2 at $0.015 per GB-month (an assumption to verify; requests and egress not measured): 10M events as Parquet is 0.45 GB, about $0.007 a month; 300M events a month is 13.5 GB a month of Parquet, and a year of retention is about 162 GB, about $2.43 a month.
  - **DuckDB at 10M events:** a per-holder, month and model sum takes 0.6 s over JSONL or over Parquet with the payload as JSON, and 0.07 to 0.09 s over Parquet with typed columns; a heavy holder's month 0.12 s over Parquet by day; the whole-school month 0.55 s. Dedupe by `idem` with `QUALIFY row_number` costs about 1.3 s extra (2.5 s in all; 8.7 s on one thread; it ran under 1, 2 and 4 GB of memory); `arg_min` ran out of memory at 1 GB; `DISTINCT ON` was worst (6 s, 8.5 GB). A full re-rate from raw (dedupe, rollup, rate) takes 2.25 s over JSONL and 1.75 s over Parquet. `read_json_auto` costs little at 10M but can infer wrongly from an unrepresentative sample, so use an explicit schema. Results matched the generator's totals with 0 mismatching cells.
  - **Typed against JSON:** promoting fields makes the same sum 7 to 8 times faster (0.06 s against 0.45 s) for about 4% more bytes, and halves memory (345 MB against 582 MB at 10M). Promote holder (`key`), `event_time`, `date`, `ingest_time`, `idem`, model, feature, region and the input, output, cached and reasoning token counts (money as DECIMAL); keep the rest of the payload, `redacted`, `text` and `postings` as JSON or a STRUCT.
  - **Rating:** prices in integer micro-dollars per 1M tokens, joined by `ASOF JOIN` on `valid_from`; the exact amount is `tokens x price` in pico-dollars, rounded to whole cents ONCE, half away from zero, no floats; re-rating twice gives identical cents (5,368,815 at 10M), checked against an independent BigInt calculation. Where to round matters: per event gave 4,057,877 cents, 24% too low (most events are under half a cent); per line 5,369,710; once per invoice (holder and period) 5,374,437 against an exact 5,374,405. Recommendation: round once per invoice and show lines for display. A price change re-rates the whole month from raw in about 2 s (a retroactive raise added $189.53 and a cut from a date subtracted $180.58 across 8,034 holders).
  - **Late events and closed periods:** the shipper names batches `batch-<first id>-<hash>`, one per event date per ship run, so a late event goes into the old event-date partition; if that day already has a `day.jsonl` the shipper reports a `ShipConflict` and the rows stay unshipped, so late events after a combine cannot ship today. With the proposed layout they go to `late-<ingest date>.jsonl` inside the event-date partition. With September invoiced on 2026-10-02, the as-issued lines (`ingest_time` before the invoice) took 2.6 s, recomputing now 2.5 s, and reading only the `late-*` files 0.44 s (55,938 late events; +$305.47 over 7,038 lines and 3,529 holders). Recommendation: put an event that arrives after its period was invoiced on the NEXT invoice as an adjustment line referencing the original period (current lines minus as-issued lines); a credit note only if the net adjustment is negative or the customer disputes it; the earliest ingest wins for duplicates.
  - **Self-describing R2:** a DuckDB-written Parquet file carries its schema and custom key/value metadata (`COPY ... KV_METADATA {schema_version, mapping_version}`). The spike builds a create-only `<prefix>/_manifest/NNNN.json` (highest number current) with the envelope version, the payload schema per version, the mapping name and versions, the DDL, the read SQL, the column meanings with their source paths and each file's sha256, bytes, rows and versions; a reader with only the folder, `node:crypto` and the `duckdb` CLI verified the hashes, read the meanings and opened the data. An event needs `payload.schema_version` (19 B raw, about 2.4 B in zstd JSONL, nothing in Parquet). Reading old and new Parquet files together after adding `reasoning_tokens` needs `union_by_name = true` (old rows get NULL); WITHOUT it DuckDB silently takes the first file's columns, so the manifest's read SQL must always carry the flag.
  - **Raw layer cost is not the obstacle:** a PII-stripped provider `usage` object plus ids (about 304 B uncompressed) adds 44.6 B per event in Parquet, about $0.007 a month per 10M events, and re-mapping from it takes about 0.18 s at 1M (about 2 s at 10M, extrapolated). The punt above is about PII stripping, not storage.
  - **Fit with the existing code:** `appendToOutbox`, `shipOutbox`, the folder store, `readEvents` and the journal builder accept the events as is (the journal adds nothing for events with no postings); refused: the envelope `event_type` enum (`validateEvent` fails at `/event_type`), `ledgerEvent` ("engine event") and the payload registry, which are the edits listed under spike 1 (`replayEvents` on usage events was not tested).
  - **Outbox insert rate (`bun:sqlite`, events/s), batch 1 / 100 / 1000:** in memory 164k / 258k / 270k; file, WAL, `synchronous=NORMAL` 21.9k / 58k / 117k; file, WAL, `synchronous=FULL` 10k / 59k / 133k (macOS fsync is optimistic, so a server will be lower). Ceiling about 100k to 130k/s batched on a small database, but it fell to about 24k/s filling 1M rows (62k/s at 100k rows) because of the random index on `idem`. An outbox row is about 686 B and rows are never pruned: 10M events a day is about 6.9 GB a day inside the transactional family database that Litestream replicates.
  - **Real shipper limits:** 1M rows across dates 9.7 s and 1.27 GB RSS (71 batches, the largest 32k events); 1M rows on one date 6.6 s and 2.1 GB; 2.5M on one date 26 s and 5.1 GB; 5M on one date failed with "Out of memory" at 6.5 GB. A date's batch is one in-memory string (about 2 KB per event, no size cap), so one date is limited to roughly 2.5M to 5M events.
  - **Recommended layout and daily job:** `schools/<school>/usage/date=YYYY-MM-DD/` with `day.parquet` (zstd) plus `late-<ingest date>.parquet`, the full envelope with promoted typed columns (47 B per event), files cut at about 100 to 500 MB or 5M rows, partitioned by date only (model, feature and region stay columns). The hourly shipper layout makes 29.5k files for 10M events against 1.3k combined and was 1.5 to 2.4 times slower locally. Daily job: combine the day's batches and dedupe by `idem` (about 2 s at 10M), write Parquet (about 5 s, 7 GB RSS), build the daily rollup, rate (under 1 s from the rollup), emit one Stripe meter event or invoice item per holder, day, model and direction with `identifier = usage:<school>:<day>:<holder>:<model>:<direction>:<revision>` (about 33k rows a day at this density), then rebuild affected closed days from the `late-*` files. About 10 s at 10M events a day on this machine, about 30 to 40 s on a 4-core box by the thread scaling (not measured end to end). Stripe's meter-event rate limit was not checked (recalled as about 1,000 a second; an assumption).
  - **First bottleneck is not DuckDB:** it is the SQLite outbox (index growth, a single writer, 6.9 GB a day in the replicated family database) and then the shipper's whole-day in-memory batch. Usage should use its own outbox file or write batches straight to the store, with a size cap.
  - **Not measured:** cold-disk reads; real R2 HTTP, listing and request costs and prices; a 10M-event single day (the run is a month of about 330k events a day); the Stripe API rate limit and meter-event emission; a shipper run at 10M rows (extrapolated). Peak scratch use was about 31 GB, deleted afterwards.
- **Spike 3, the `track` path as a usage wallet: partly feasible.** atm is a correct, race-free credit wallet for LLM usage if the gateway batches; as a per-call wallet it has a hard ceiling and gaps. Branch `claude/atm-usage-track-spike` (head `9d815769c`, not merged, not a PR): `tests/spikes/usage-track-harness.ts` and `usage-track.test.ts` (9 tests; the full suite, `tsc`, Biome and `knip` passed on it). Measured on an Apple M5 Pro (15 cores, 48 GB, macOS APFS, Bun 1.4.4) that was busy (load 6 to 13), so absolute numbers are about 2x noisy and the ratios are the reliable part; SQLite is a file in WAL with `synchronous=NORMAL` and the real `atm_rows` layout and revision check.
  - **Single-thread throughput, one transaction per `track` (read rows, run the engine, diff, write under the revision check):** the pure engine is about 125k to 145k tracks/s (6 to 8 us). With SQLite: one holder 15.6k to 23.8k/s (p50 36 to 42 us, p99 65 to 277 us); 1,000 holders round-robin 19k to 21k/s; 20,000 holders with a heavy-tailed pick (top 10 get 39%) 15k to 17k/s; the quantity (1 or 1,000,000 credits) makes no difference. `:memory:` 35k/s; `synchronous=FULL` 11.5k to 12.6k/s (the commit costs about 50 us; Linux fsync was not measured). A track costs about 42 to 65 us: read 2 to 7, decode 2 to 3, engine 8 to 12, the diff (`rowChangesFromMutation`) 11 to 13 (more than the engine), write 2 to 6, BEGIN/COMMIT 16 to 28. A lean holder has 3 rows (3 read, 1 upserted); one with a rollover and a pack has 5.
  - **The audited path, a hold then a settle per LLM call, with `recordEngineCommand` writing the outbox in the same transaction:** 3.6k to 4.2k calls/s (about 8k commands/s), p50 176 to 199 us, p99 2.6 to 3.3 ms (cause unconfirmed); the outbox write adds 35 to 40 us per command, and an event is about 2.6 KB.
  - **Hot-holder contention, K processes on one SQLite file:** more writers on one holder make it SLOWER: with the handler's `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` pattern, K=1 23.3k/s, K=2 10.7k, K=4 5.2k, K=8 4.3k; spreading the writers over different holders does not help (K=8 spread still 2.5k/s), because SQLite allows one writer per file. With the optimistic pattern (read, decide, write under the revision check, retry on `StaleRevision`) on one holder, K=8 has 78% to 80% stale writes and 3.6 to 3.9 retries per track; when the read and the write are 1 ms apart (a remote or async store) one holder tops out near 1/RTT, about 700 commits/s, with 87% stale at K=8. No run lost an update (revision equals commits, balance equals the total tracked). Ceiling for one holder: about 15k to 23k tracks/s from ONE in-process writer; 4k to 10k/s across processes on a shared file; a few hundred/s with a 1 ms window. For a gateway: route each customer to a single writer (same process or shard), or batch.
  - **Batching (one holder, 137-credit calls):** one summed `track` costs about one track (50 to 160 us) however many calls it covers, so the speedup is about M (about 20x at 10 calls, 180x at 100, 2,100x at 1,000; noisy). Exact boundary behaviour for a balance of 100 and 13 calls of 10: per call with `reject`, 10 applied, 3 rejected, balance 0; one batch of 130 with `reject` rejects the WHOLE batch (nothing deducted); with `cap` it applies 100 and drops the other 30 silently (`status: "applied"`, no `remaining` field: the caller must compute requested minus the sum of `deltas`); with `overflow` all 130 apply and the balance is -30. atm's own rows are `usage_allowed: false`; a customer-row `overage_allowed: [{feature_id, enabled: true}]` allows overage without a catalog change, and `spend_limits` with `overage_limit: 20` makes `reject` refuse the batch and `cap` end at -20. `check` returns `{allowed, reason, limitType, requiredBalance, ...}`, no balance, and writes nothing. Batching loses per-call audit (one revision and one command id per batch), per-call idempotency (the engine has none: "Dedup is the writer's job") and partial-failure isolation. Usage that already happened cannot be un-served, so a batch needs `overflow` or an overage-allowed row, with a `check` or a held `track` first when the gateway must block. Recommended rule: flush per holder every about 250 ms or at 100 buffered calls (whichever first), one `track` of the summed quantity; flush on shutdown and when `check` shows a low balance; send the raw per-call usage event (call id, time, model, tokens, credit cost, the batch's `commandId`, an idempotency key) to R2 independently, so atm holds the balance and the aggregate and R2 is the audit and billing source.
  - **Overage is not reportable yet.** `track`'s result is `{status, reason, deltas[], deductions[], fundingFeatureId, ...}` with no overage field and no remaining balance; overage is a negative row balance, so the quantity now is `-(sum of the negative customerEntitlements balances)` (rollovers never go negative) and per call it must be derived from `deltas` and the prior rows. The ledger cannot log it: `mutationToEvents` throws "a spend books a hold first: track with a lock" for a plain `track` (only a held `track` makes `credit.held`, and its units include overage with nothing separating them), "a finalize above its lock deducts more; not mapped to an event" (an LLM call that costs more than its estimate cannot be settled and logged), and "credits are whole units" for a fractional hold. `credit.low` and `credit.depleted` do fire in a usage loop (opt-in via `lowCreditThreshold`; 100 credits and five held calls of 40, 40, 19, 1, 30 end at -30 with five `credit.held`, one `credit.low` and one `credit.depleted`), but no event fires once the balance is already at or below 0.
  - **A reset forgives overage and the ledger cannot book it.** A `reset` at the cycle end sets -30 back to the grant (with `persist_free_overage` on it gives 999,970 instead); `mutationToEvents` throws "a reset cannot add more than it refills (-30)" in both cases, and in `handleStripeEvent` a throw inside `decideAndRun` becomes a `rejected` refusal. So a renewal invoice for a holder in overage is probably REFUSED; this is inferred from the proven throw and was not run end to end through the handler, so verify before relying on overage rows (it only arises when a caller sets `overage_allowed` on the customer row).
  - **Missing for a "report overage to a Stripe meter" job (none of it built):** a per-period overage reader (the negative balance now, plus the amount at reset); a durable idempotent cursor (period start, last reported quantity or high-water mark, the Stripe meter-event identifier); a reset path that records overage before forgiving it and that the ledger can book; an overage field on `track` results and events; a per-call usage idempotency key on the engine side; settling above the lock; a mapping from credit units to meter units.
  - **Decimals and units:** the engine's deduction uses `decimal.js` and is exact (`0.1` ten times from 1 gives exactly 0; 1000 x 0.137 from 1000 gives 863, where a float sum leaves 137.00000000000043), but the stored balance is a JS double: `1e-6` from `1e12` returns `applied` and the balance stays `1e12` (the deduction is silently lost); `1e-21` from 1 is `rejected` (below `CREDIT_RATE_EPSILON` 1e-10); integers are exact to 2^53; NaN and Infinity are rejected by the schema; negative values refund; zero is a no-op; ledger postings reject non-integers. Recommendation: track WHOLE units (for example 1 credit = 1,000 tokens), round up per call (or per batch for large counts), keep every balance under 2^53, and keep the exact token counts in the R2 usage events.
  - **Not measured:** Linux fsync or NVMe; a remote or async store (D1, Postgres, a Durable Object; only a simulated 1 ms window); a multi-thread engine; a quiet machine; reset end to end through the handler; outbox growth, WAL checkpoints and vacuum over a long run; entity-scoped or multi-feature holders; rate cards or credit-system pricing.
- **Design decisions already taken for the R2 layer** (2026-11-05, from the agentlake and Tares comparison; measured in spike 2): typed quantities as real columns for the hot fields (holder, time, idem, model, usage_feature, the token counts) with the rest as JSON; a manifest written beside the data (envelope and payload schema versions, mapping name and version, DDL, file hashes) so a reader with only the folder and `duckdb` can understand it. The redacted raw layer is the punted item above.

### Credit ledger depth (from the Orb, Metronome and OpenMeter comparison)
- Not built: drawdown by priority and by cost basis, and a `pending_payment`-style hold on credits whose invoice is unpaid. atm draws by expiry then oldest grant.

### Compared with open-source billing (Lago, OpenMeter, Flexprice, Kill Bill, Polar)
Written 2026-11-05 from memory of their public docs, not read or run; check a row before acting on it. What atm lacks that they have, and where it stands here:
- Metered usage rating and invoicing: ruled out above.
- Prepaid wallets and auto top-up (Lago, Flexprice): not built. The Autumn monorepo has an `auto-topup` package that atm does not use.
- Other payment providers (Lago, Kill Bill, Flexprice): atm is Stripe only. Not planned.
- Own subscription lifecycle (proration, trials, dunning, coupons): left to Stripe, see Ruled out.
- Tax, invoice PDFs, the customer portal itself (atm only builds the configuration for tiers sold in quantities: `portalConfigOf`), admin UI, REST API, multi-tenant auth: left to Stripe and the caller.
- Revenue analytics (MRR and similar): only the ledger export; the Parquet export and any dashboard are not built.
- Expiry alerts by the clock: see Alerts above.
- Rebuild from the log: `replayEvents` now reproduces what `handleStripeEvent` wrote (see Review above); `simulate` and a rebuild of a subject's rows from a stored log are not built.
- Reuse outside B4 Uni: see "Decouple, the cheap parts" (prefix, `family_id`, `env`, menu limits), and the Worker handler blocked on `ajv`.
- SOC 2, a DPA and subprocessor list: none; the caller writes them.

- Rebuilding Stripe Billing's subscription lifecycle (checkout, proration, renewals, retries, trials) or Autumn's server: Stripe Billing owns that, atm owns credits.
