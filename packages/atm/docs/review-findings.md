## The eight-slice review

(The section "Review" of the roadmap, whole.)

All eight slices were reviewed by sub-agents and the findings fixed slice by slice (see the PRs for each fix). Decisions that came out of it:

- **Handler `schoolId` binding.** The webhook handler's deps carry a required `schoolId`, the school an endpoint and its secret belong to. An event naming another school is refused (422 `school_mismatch`); an event with no `b4u_school` at all is answered 200 `ignored`; half-filled metadata stays 422 `missing_metadata`; an unmapped event type answers 200 `ignored`; a payment linked to more than one invoice answers 422 `ambiguous_payment`.
- **Alerts only for `track` and `finalize` draws.** `credit.low` and `credit.depleted` follow customer activity (holds and spends), not refund or dispute clawbacks or admin edits. They are judged on availability including holds. atm does not remember it already alerted, so the family service dedupes per holder, feature and grant or cycle.
- **`FolderStore` reserved names are case-insensitive**, and both object stores validate keys and ranges more strictly.
- **Equal-second membership ordering.** Membership events that carry the same second now have a defined order (see the PR for the rule).
- **`deterministicUlid` is exported** so a caller can derive `eventId` as the handler does.
- **Replay of the log works** (#29, merged into `dev`). It had failed even on a plain create, renew, cancel of a one-feature tier. Three causes, three fixes: event ids are time-based, so a `reset` at the period start sorted before the `applyBillingPlan` at payment time (replay now runs each subject's commands in revision order, `replayOrder`); commands that moved no credits were not logged (each is now a `plan.changed` with its command and mutation, and every handler event carries `payload.complete`); and each per-feature event recorded only its own slice of the mutation (every event now records the whole mutation, with `payload.slice` naming the changes it is made from). The replay golden gained the `plan.changed` events; the envelope is unchanged; the `credit.*` payload schemas gain optional `slice` and `complete`. Owner decisions: `payload.mutation.changes` holds all changes and a reader uses `payload.slice`; `plan.changed` for every command that moves no credits (no `plan.attached`); replay tolerates a revision gap in a log whose writer marked `complete` (logging grants-only writes would close it). **Logs written by 0.3.0** are read, not rewritten: mutations are reassembled by `mutation.id` and checked as a subset of what the engine decides; a log with a revision gap stops with `ReplayGapError` (the lost cycle-end update cannot be told from a grants-only write); a log without one replays and reports `lossySubjects`. What remains: a 0.3.0 log cannot be rebuilt past its first gap. Closing that needs the stored rows at that revision (the handler's `atm_rows`) or a one-off migration that appends the missing commands, neither built.

## Raise rule: owner decision and known limits

- [ ] Owner decision (`policy.upgradeGrant`): the invoice of the switch itself still prorates over the OLD cycle (a monthly sparkle-4 x2 moved to yearly x4 grants 128 = 192 x 20/30, though the new yearly period starts at the change). Whether a switch should grant the whole 192 is not decided.
- [ ] Known limits of the raise baseline (`GrantsState.quantity`): two raise invoices delivered out of order (3 to 4 first, then 2 to 3) grant 3 in total where the dev code before #23 gave 2 + 2, because the late one sees the credits already at 4; atm has no ordering guarantee for invoices, and closing it needs per-invoice history. A refund of a raise does not roll `state.quantity` back (same as before). `raiseUnits` now throws on bad input (NaN, a null `oldQuantity` from plain JavaScript, a negative or fractional count, `cycleEnd` before `cycleStart`, a cycle in seconds against `at` in milliseconds), with a message naming the field; the seconds-against-milliseconds check is a heuristic (a magnitude gap of 100 or more). `prorationPair` throws on a non-array `lines`, a non-function `isTierPrice`, and a tier proration line with a non-integer `quantity`, a non-finite `amount` or a bad period (other lines are ignored). The checks are in (this PR).

## Open findings of the review of #22 to #24

- [ ] Open findings of the 2026-11-05 review of #22 to #24 (`code-review high`; its fixes are in the PR that adds this line):
  - #22 (`portalConfigOf`): the full finding, with what was verified in the sandbox, is in `docs/stripe-findings.md` ("Portal limits found in the sandbox: add-ons and older prices").
  - #23 (raise rule): a tier change while a decrease is pending is tested now (`tests/billing-raise.test.ts`): the baseline is what the credits hold, so sparkle-2 x4 (8 visits, decrease to x2 pending) moving to sparkle-4 x2 grants no visits (it holds 8 already) and to sparkle-4 x4 grants the 8 more, prorated. That is less than Stripe charged for (it prices the move from the lower count), by design: the member keeps the higher count's credits until the renewal. `prorationPair` picks the credit line with no tier or interval check and falls back to the first credit when the charge has no `periodStart`, so a caller that reads `oldQuantity` can get a stale pair's.
  - #24 (adoption; owner decisions): an older lot with no expiry, or one still in the future, becomes a rollover row even when the grant has no rollover; a lapsed newest lot keeps its `adjustment` and `additional_balance`; an add-on invoice with some lots removed and some not is read as not removed; an add-on lot with `every: "once"` is accepted onto a refilling row; `cycleStart` is cycleEnd minus one UTC interval, which differs from Stripe's period start at a month end. An endpoint left on an older API version now has its b4u invoices refused with 422 and retried by Stripe; the dashboard is the only signal.

- [ ] Differences the import cannot hide, from the #24 review (the first group is in `UPGRADING.md`): a session gift bought after the import expires after `policy.giftSessionMonths` (6), where the caller's lot has no expiry without rollover; a refund of a pre-import invoice needs rows in BOTH `atm_payment_index` and `atm_invoice_index`; the caller grants with `school_billing.time_zone` while the import uses `menu.timeZone` (they can differ after a zone change; the result is a refusal, not a silent error); a downgrade not yet paid reads as a higher quantity from the newest lot's allowance; if a tier ever lists two grants of one feature and interval, atm sums them where the caller's lot id keeps only the first (check that menu validation forbids it).
